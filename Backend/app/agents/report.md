# Effects of Climate Change on Global Temperatures and Extreme Weather Events

## Executive Summary

This report synthesizes the findings regarding the effects of climate change on global temperatures and extreme weather events. Key observations include a significant increase in global average temperatures since the late 19th century, with projections indicating further rises and an increase in the frequency and intensity of extreme weather events such as hurricanes, floods, and droughts. The data reflects an urgent need for enhanced global climate policies and adaptation strategies, particularly in vulnerable regions.

## Introduction

The research question examines how climate change impacts global temperatures and the occurrence of extreme weather events. Recent trends indicate that human activities, primarily the burning of fossil fuels, have accelerated warming, affecting global weather patterns and intensifying the occurrence of extremeweather phenomena.

## Key Findings

### Global Temperature Increases
- **Temperature Rise**: Since 1850, global average temperatures have increased by approximately 1.1°C (around 2°F), with the warmest years on record occurring in the last decade, including 2024 being noted as the warmest year at 2.32°F (1.29°C) above the 20th-century average [according to NOAA](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/climate-change-global-temperature).
- **Acceleration of Warming**: The rate of warming has accelerated, showing an increase from approximately 0.11°F (0.06°C) per decade since 1850 to more than 0.36°F (0.20°C) per decade since 1975.
- **Future Projections**: Without significant reductions in greenhouse gas emissions, global temperatures may rise an additional 4°C to 5°C (7.2°F to 9°F) by the century's end [as reported by the Center for Science Education](https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/climate-change-impacts/predictions-future-global-climate).

### Extreme Weather Events
- **Correlation with Temperature**: Rising global temperatures correlate significantly with the frequency and intensity of extreme weather events, particularly at low and mid-latitudes. About 77% of extreme weather events are estimated to be exacerbated by human-induced climate change [according to Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/mapped-how-climate-change-affects-extreme-weather-around-the-world).
- **Types of Extreme Events**: 
  - Increased frequency of heatwaves and severe temperature events has been documented as temperatures rise.
  - Enhanced evaporation due to higher temperatures leads to increased humidity and rainfall intensity, exacerbating flooding risks.
  - Hurricanes and coastal storms are intensifying, with major hurricanes increasing in intensity by approximately 5% per decade [as noted by Climate Central](https://www.climatecentral.org/climate-matters/hurricane-intensity-and-impacts).

### Regional Variations
- **Geographic Disparities**: Local and regional factors greatly influence specific temperature extremes. For example, the Arctic is warming about twice asfast as the global average, leading to more intense extreme weather events in affected regions [as reported by IPCC](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-11).
- **Vulnerable Populations**: Regions with lower socioeconomic development are particularly susceptible to the adverse effects of climate change, with increased frequency of extreme weather events such as heatwaves and heavy rainfall [according to the Climate Risk Index 2026](https://www.germanwatch.org/en/cri).

## Detailed Analysis

### Relationships between Temperature and Weather Events
The evidence underscores a strong relationship between rising temperatures and extreme weather. As global temperatures increase, several consequential patterns emerge:
- **Heatwaves and Intensity**: The frequency and duration of heatwaves have risen significantly, with projected increases as global temperatures continue to climb.
- **Increased Rainfall and Floods**: Rainfall events that typically occur once in a decade are now projected to have a much higher probability, with human-driven sea level rise exacerbating coastal flooding risks.
- **Economics and Extreme Events**: Economic impacts stemming from extreme weather are escalating, with the average number of billion-dollar disasters—including floods—surging from three annually in the 1980s to about 20 in the last decade [as stated by Climate Central](https://www.climatecentral.org/climate-matters/mid-year-billion-dollar-disasters).

### Mitigation and Adaptation Strategies
Current global measures to mitigate climate change include technological advancements in renewable energy and carbon capture, alongside international policy efforts like the Conference of Parties (COP) negotiations. Despite these efforts, persistent extreme weather events suggest that further action is necessary. Adaptive strategies, particularly for health and infrastructure, are vital for enhancing resilience [according to the UNDP](https://climatepromise.undp.org/news-and-stories/what-climate-change-mitigation-and-why-it-urgent).

## Key Insights

1. **Significant Temperature Rise**: Global temperatures have risen steadily, with alarming projections for future increases unless immediate action is taken.
2. **Linkage to Extreme Weather**: There is a well-established connection between increasing temperatures and the severity and frequency of extreme weatherevents.
3. **Urgency for Climate Policy**: Effective climate mitigation and adaptation strategies are crucial for protecting vulnerable populations and ecosystems from the escalating impacts of climate change.

## Limitations and Uncertainties

- Variability in regional impacts highlights the complexity of climate responses, with some areas more affected than others.
- Continued uncertainty exists regarding the effectiveness of current mitigation strategies; despite progress in emissions reductions, extreme weather patterns persist.
- The potential for “worst-case” scenarios remains a critical area for future investigation, particularly regarding greenhouse gas emissions and resultant climatic thresholds.

## Conclusion

The evidence robustly supports that climate change is causing significant increases in global temperatures, which in turn intensifies extreme weather events. The interplay between rising temperatures and severe weather patterns necessitates urgent global action in mitigation and adaptation efforts to safeguardhuman and environmental health.
